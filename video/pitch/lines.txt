b0|This is AI Playbook for Schools: a responsible AI guide that helps school staff choose tools that protect student privacy and work for every student.
b1|School staff are the experts on their students, and AI is already part of their work: about six in ten teachers in the U.S., and two in three teachers in Canada, use AI tools. Here's the problem. Every week brings a new AI tool to judge: what it costs, whether it's fair, and whether student data stays safe. Each school has to answer those questions on its own, often with only the vendor's marketing to go on.
b2|Elizabeth Larkin is an AI-native developer and analyst with Stone Arch Collective, a Minnesota cooperative of AI practitioners who help schools and organizations use AI well. While she was helping high school leaders in a Minnesota public school district plan how to use AI, they told her that they and their staff spend a lot of time checking AI tools against their district's AI policy. So we built a playbook that starts with their role, and puts the facts side by side, so they can choose tools faster and spend that time on students.
d1|Special educators know what their students need. Pick your role, and the tools built for that work move to the front.
d2|Filter by cost and by the systems your district already runs, so nobody starts from scratch. Not sure how a filter works? Tap the i.
d3|Every badge is a fact from the company's own website, explained in plain words. Tap it to see exactly what they promise, one topic at a time.
d4|The scouting report shows where your data goes: where it's stored, and where it may be sent to be processed, even when the company doesn't say. It also lists what a tool doesn't offer, so staff can ask the right questions before any student information goes in.
d5|If a company hasn't published a fact, we don't guess. It's listed under Under review, and we recheck every tool together.
d6|Copy-ready prompts protect student privacy by design. And as staff rate tools, the ones people in your role trust most rise for your role.
t1|Behind the scenes, AI does the heavy lifting, and people stay in charge. Claude Code helps us research and recheck each company's own pages, and a person approves every fact. Voice reviews become text through the browser's own speech recognition, and a person checks every review before it's shown.
b4|Here's the systemic change. Vetting stops being something every school repeats alone and becomes a shared, public resource: one recheck helps every school, including the ones without a tech team. Publishing what companies haven't shared puts pressure on vendors to share more. And rankings come from staff in each role, with fair-average rules that stop gaming, and no paid placement, ever.
b5|Educators decide: they rate, review and shape the rankings, and every fact links to its source. When something's wrong, we fix it in the open.
b6|Responsible AI isn't only about how a tool is built. It's also about how it's chosen. Every AI tool a teacher picks ends up in front of students: their writing, their questions, sometimes their data. When school staff can see the facts first, like where student data goes, whether the tool works on every device and in every language, and whether it's accessible, they choose tools that protect students and work for all of them. And when every school can make those choices, more students get safe, fair access to good learning tools. That's what UN Sustainable Development Goal 4 asks for: quality education, with equal access for every learner.
b7|In future phases, we're adding two enhancements. Members will be able to add their district's AI policy, so the playbook fits their rules. And a prompt builder: pick a tool, type what you want to do, and AI builds a prompt that fits that tool, designed to keep student data private. Stone Arch Collective plans to lead a pilot with schools, starting in Minnesota. As we learn how AI school policies and guidance are applied in other countries, including in Canada, we hope to grow beyond the U.S. We're asking you for introductions to schools and educators who want to help shape it. {AI Playbook for Schools.} {Teach smart.
